# Lighting and Composition

## Lighting Setups

### Portrait Lighting Patterns

**Rembrandt Lighting**
- Key light at approximately 45 degrees to camera axis, slightly above eye level
- Produces a small triangle of light on the shadow side of the face
- Creates dimension and drama; ideal for editorial and luxury brand portraits
- Fill light ratio: typically 2:1 to 4:1

**Butterfly Lighting (Paramount Lighting)**
- Key light directly in front of and above the subject's face
- Creates a small butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose
- Flattering for angular faces and high-fashion beauty work
- Minimizes texture; use diffused source for skin retouching advantage

**Split Lighting**
- Key light at 90 degrees to the subject
- Half the face is illuminated, half in shadow
- High contrast, dramatic, editorial feel
- Best for moody campaigns or creative brand positioning

**Loop Lighting**
- Key light slightly off-axis (30 to 45 degrees) and above eye level
- Creates a small nose shadow that loops toward the shadow side
- Universally flattering; standard for corporate and commercial portraiture
- Soft shadows maintain dimension without harshness

**Broad Lighting**
- Key light illuminates the side of the face turned toward the camera
- Widens the appearance of the face
- Effective for narrow or angular faces
- Common in beauty and fashion editorial

### Product Photography Lighting

**Softbox Lighting**
- Primary tool for product photography
- Position at 45 degrees to product surface for even illumination with subtle modeling
- Use larger softbox relative to product size for softer shadows
- Grid attachments control spill when photographing reflective surfaces

**Ring Light Lighting**
- Provides even, shadowless illumination
- Ideal for flat lays, jewelry, and cosmetics where product detail is paramount
- Creates signature circular catchlight in reflective surfaces
- Use diffusion dome to soften harsh reflections on metallic products

**Light Tent / Softbox Tent**
- Encloses product in diffused light from all angles
- Eliminates hard shadows and reflections
- Best for e-commerce catalog photography where consistency is critical
- Use with adjustable LED panels for color temperature control

### Lighting Ratios and Exposure

| Ratio | Look | Use Case |
|-------|------|----------|
| 1:1 | Flat, even, shadowless | E-commerce, catalog |
| 2:1 | Soft dimension, minimal shadow | Corporate headshots, product |
| 4:1 | Moderate contrast, visible shadow | Editorial portraits, lifestyle |
| 8:1 | High contrast, dramatic | Fashion, luxury, creative campaigns |

## Composition Rules

### Rule of Thirds
- Divide frame into nine equal sections with two horizontal and two vertical lines
- Place key subjects or horizon lines along these lines or at their intersections
- Creates visual tension and interest; avoids static centered composition

### Golden Ratio (Phi Grid)
- Based on the Fibonacci spiral (1:1.618 ratio)
- More organic than rule of thirds; guides the eye naturally through the frame
- Particularly effective for single-subject product shots and portrait orientation
- Use spiral overlay in post-production to check alignment

### Negative Space
- Intentional empty space around the subject
- Communicates luxury, minimalism, and clarity
- Allows text or logo placement in marketing assets
- Balance is critical — too much negative space reads as empty; too little reads as cluttered

### Leading Lines
- Use architectural elements, product edges, or model posture to lead the eye toward the subject
- Diagonal lines create dynamic energy; horizontal lines create calm and stability
- Converging lines add depth and perspective

### Symmetry and Patterns
- Perfect symmetry conveys precision, order, and luxury
- Broken symmetry (intentional disruption) creates visual intrigue
- Repeating patterns reinforce brand consistency in series photography

## Camera Angles and Perspectives

### Standard Angles

| Angle | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Eye level | Camera at subject eye height | Portraits, human-centered campaigns |
| High angle | Camera above subject, looking down | Product flat lays, hierarchy communication |
| Low angle | Camera below subject, looking up | Hero products, architecture, power dynamics |
| Dutch angle | Tilted camera horizon | Editorial, creative, disruptive campaigns |
| Overhead / Flat lay | Camera perpendicular to surface | Product collections, layouts, food |

### Lens Selection for Perspective

- **50mm equivalent:** Natural perspective, minimal distortion, standard for general product work
- **85mm equivalent:** Compressed perspective, flattering for portraits, isolates subject
- **24-35mm equivalent:** Wide angle for environmental context, beware of edge distortion on products
- **100mm macro:** Extreme detail for texture, jewelry, cosmetics, small product features
- **24-70mm zoom:** Versatile for run-and-gun campaign days; minimize zooming during composition to avoid perspective shift

### Depth of Field

- **Deep DOF (f/8 to f/16):** Everything in focus; essential for product catalog and flat lays
- **Moderate DOF (f/2.8 to f/5.6):** Subject sharp, background softly blurred; lifestyle and environmental product
- **Shallow DOF (f/1.4 to f/2):** Extreme subject isolation; fashion, beauty, creative portrait work

## Background and Surface Selection

### Background Materials

- **Seamless paper:** Infinite sweep, color-matched to brand palette; standard for studio product
- **Canvas / Muslin:** Textured, organic; suitable for lifestyle and artisanal brand positioning
- **Plexiglass / Acrylic:** Reflective, modern; high-tech, beauty, and luxury product photography
- **Wood / Stone / Concrete:** Natural or architectural surfaces; lifestyle and environmental context
- **Chroma key / Green screen:** Post-production replacement; use only when background is entirely unknown or variable

### Surface Selection

- Match surface material to product material (e.g., marble for luxury beauty, reclaimed wood for sustainable brands)
- Consider color temperature interaction — warm surfaces warm cool products, and vice versa
- Use surface props sparingly; every object in frame must serve the brand narrative
- Test surfaces under actual shooting lighting, not just ambient room light
