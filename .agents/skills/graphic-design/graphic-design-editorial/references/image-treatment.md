# Image Treatment

## Image Placement and Bleed

- Place images within the column grid or span across gutters intentionally.
- Full-bleed images extend to the trim edge; account for bleed in image export.
- Keep critical content (faces, text in photos) inside the safe margin (typically 3–5 mm inside trim).
- Align image edges to grid lines or gutters whenever possible; avoid floating images without alignment.

## Captions and Credits

- Captions sit directly below the image or in a dedicated caption zone.
- Use caption width equal to image width or column width; do not let captions span multiple columns unless the image does.
- Photo credits are smaller (6–8 pt), set in sans or caption style, separated from caption text by a rule or space.
- Caption punctuation follows the publication style guide; be consistent across the issue or book.

## Image-to-Text Ratios

- Editorial spreads typically use 40–60% image, 40–60% text depending on density.
- Feature articles may invert the ratio (60–80% image) for visual impact.
- Captions and white space count toward the non-text area.
- Avoid large text blocks without visual relief on screen-based reading; insert images or pull quotes every 300–500 words.

## CMYK Conversion for Print Images

- Convert RGB images to CMYK before placement in print layout.
- Choose CMYK profiles based on press standard: GRACoL, FOGRA, SWOP.
- Check for out-of-gamut colors after conversion; adjust or replace problematic hues.
- Spot colors require explicit channel assignment; do not rely on automatic conversion for Pantone or HKS.

## Accessibility: Alt Text Equivalents for Digital

- Every meaningful image requires concise, descriptive alt text (1–2 sentences).
- Decorative images use empty alt (`alt=""`) or CSS background images.
- Complex charts and infographics need extended descriptions near the image or in a linked appendix.
- Alt text should convey the image's purpose in context, not just its literal content.
