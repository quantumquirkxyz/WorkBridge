# Color Palette Frameworks Reference

## Framework Types

### Brand-Centric
- Built around existing brand colors.
- Expands primary and secondary colors into a full system.
- Maintains brand recognition across applications.

### Audience-Centric
- Chosen based on audience expectations and cultural context.
- May prioritize accessibility and readability over brand match.

### Channel-Centric
- Optimized for specific mediums: print, digital, outdoor.
- Accounts for color reproduction limitations and strengths.

## Palette Construction

### Primary Palette
- One to three dominant colors.
- Used for logo, headlines, and primary actions.

### Secondary Palette
- Three to six supporting colors.
- Used for variety, emphasis, and section differentiation.

### Accent Palette
- One to two highlight colors.
- Used sparingly for calls to action and key details.

### Neutral Palette
- Four to six grayscale values.
- Used for body text, backgrounds, and borders.

## Semantic Color Roles
- **Success:** positive confirmation, completion.
- **Warning:** caution, attention needed.
- **Error:** failure, destructive action.
- **Info:** neutral information, tips.

## Accessibility Constraints
- All text-on-background pairs must meet WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Do not rely solely on hue to convey information.
- Provide color-blind-safe alternatives for data visualization.

## Format Specifications
- **HEX:** web and digital design.
- **RGB:** screen design and digital production.
- **CMYK:** print production.
- **Pantone:** brand-critical print matching.
- **OKLCH:** perceptually uniform digital palettes.
