# Brand Governance Reference

## Governance Levels

### Centralized
- Single brand team owns all applications.
- Strong consistency but slower throughput.
- Best for: large enterprises, regulated industries.

### Federated
- Central brand team defines rules; local teams apply with autonomy.
- Balance of consistency and speed.
- Best for: multi-product companies, global brands.

### Decentralized
- Local teams own brand applications with minimal central oversight.
- Fast but inconsistent.
- Best for: startups, small organizations.

## Approval Workflow

1. **Request:** submit brand application with context and mockups.
2. **Review:** brand team checks against guidelines.
3. **Feedback:** provide specific corrections and rationale.
4. **Revision:** requester updates application.
5. **Approval:** brand team signs off.
6. **Archive:** store approved application in brand repository.

## Usage Rules

### Logo Usage
- Minimum clear space must be maintained.
- Logo must not be altered, stretched, or recolored without approval.
- Approved variations only.

### Color Usage
- Use approved palette values exactly.
- Do not create new brand colors without approval.
- Maintain contrast for readability.

### Typography Usage
- Use approved typefaces for brand-facing materials.
- Fallback fonts allowed for body copy only with approval.
- Do not substitute display typefaces without review.

### Imagery Usage
- Use approved photography and illustration styles.
- Do not use unlicensed stock without review.
- Maintain consistent treatment across channels.

## Co-Branding
- Define hierarchy when multiple brands appear together.
- Specify clear space between brand marks.
- Define which brand takes precedence in different contexts.

## Exception Process
- Document rationale for exception requests.
- Require approval from brand owner and legal if needed.
- Log exceptions for future guideline updates.
